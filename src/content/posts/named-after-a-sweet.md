Aleywa Studios is named after alewa, a boiled-sugar sweet many kids in Ghana grew up eating. This post sets down how the name, the square full stop and our colours came from it, in the same order we decided them.

### A name you can say first

We wanted a name that says what we are for before it says what we do. Alewa is short, it is said the way it is written, and it carries a memory: a sweet made by hand, cut into pieces, and shared. A studio that decides a brand and a website together needs a name a client can say first, without spelling it.

### Round letters, one square stop

Alewa is cut, not moulded. That gave us the idea in one line: round letters, one square stop. The wordmark is lower case, with round letters and flat-ended strokes, ending in a square raspberry full stop. The stop is the hand-cut piece, and it ends every headline on this site. Every piece takes one risk, and the stop is ours.

### Colour sampled, then turned down

The colours came from photographs of real alewa, then we lowered the vividness: paper, ink, stone, one raspberry for the stop, and one berry for the single dark surface. A hot pink failed, because white on it is only 3.7 to 1. We kept the raspberry for the stop and the berry for the one dark surface, and wrote the values down so anyone can check them.

### What we gave up

We gave up bubbly candy imagery and the usual twisted wrapper. The sweet is the idea, not a picture on the page. What is left is a name, a square, and colours you can trace back to the thing itself.
