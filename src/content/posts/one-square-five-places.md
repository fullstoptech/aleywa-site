One raspberry square ends our wordmark, cuts through our card, counts our letters and fills a billboard. This post follows that one square through five places, and how one device carries a brand identity.

### The wordmark and the card

The square starts as a full stop: it ends the wordmark and every headline. On the card it stops being printed. A 9 mm square is cut through the paper face, the size of the stop, and stacked, the edges show a raspberry line. The same measure, once as ink and once as a hole.

### Letterhead, envelope, billboard

A letterhead carries a column of squares that counts its letters. An envelope has a square window. On a billboard the square fills the field at scale, one deliberate risk, disciplined everywhere else. Each piece takes the square at the size its material allows, and changes nothing else about it.

### The wall

In some cities, billboards are read in pieces, pasted side by side. We designed a wall that reads one word to a row, the square ending each line. Five places, one square, no second device. If a sixth place cannot take the square honestly, it does not take a substitute.

### What we gave up

We gave up printing the stop on the card, and every second device we sketched. One square, carried properly, is the identity. A second device would only be decoration.
