[44.0] One square stop
[18.0] The wordmark is lower case, with round letters and flat-ended strokes, ending in a square raspberry full stop. The stop is the hand-cut piece of candy, and it ends every headline. Every piece takes one risk.
[18.0] We gave up bubbly candy imagery and the usual twisted wrapper.

[44.0] Drawn by hand
[18.0] We set the name in 45 typefaces. None carried the idea, and several read as default faces. So we drew the wordmark.
[18.0] We gave up a ready-made typeface, and a cut on the e.
[44.0] Spaced by measuring
[18.0] We set the space between letters by measuring the closest distance between neighbouring outlines, not the boxes around them. The y and w had the most empty space, so we tightened that pair to about 3.4 units after comparing four values. Small sizes get their own drawing.
[13.0] The numbers are rounded and on our own measure.

[44.0] Colour sampled,
[44.0] then turned down
[18.0] The colours came from photographs of real alewa, then we lowered the vividness: paper, ink, stone, one raspberry for the stop, and one berry for the single dark surface. A hot pink failed: white on it is only 3.7 to 1.
[18.0] We gave up bright pink as a ground.
